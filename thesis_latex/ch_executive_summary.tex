\section*{Introduction}
Deep neural networks (DNNs) dominate modern computing across numerous applications. However, their immense computational cost requires billions of operations for a single inference pass.
While general-purpose processors and GPUs deliver high throughput,
their rigid memory hierarchies often struggle to meet the stringent
energy-efficiency requirements of edge devices.
This bottleneck motivated the rise of spatial architectures (SAs), custom edge accelerators that exploit massive data-level parallelism. An SA's efficiency relies critically on its mapping: the schedule determining how loop iterations are tiled, ordered, and distributed across the hardware.
Even for a single convolutional layer on a moderately complex SA, the
resulting set of all valid mappings, the \emph{map space}, exceeds $10^{19}$ candidates, driving the need for analytical cost models and automated exploration tools.
Yet the classic \emph{layer-by-layer} execution model
introduces a critical constraint: after one layer completes, its
output tensor is written to off-chip DRAM, only to be read
back when the next layer begins.
Because DRAM accesses are highly energy-expensive, this intermediate-activation traffic frequently dominates the energy budget and degrades latency. Layer fusion addresses this by computing multiple consecutive layers in a single interleaved schedule.
By tiling the execution, intermediate activations are produced and consumed entirely within on-chip buffer. Hardware implementations, such as DepFiN~\cite{goetschalckx2023depfin}, adopt a \emph{depth-first}
inter-layer scheduling: the execution remains sequential across layers,
but the SA computes an intermediate tile buffered exclusively
on-chip and immediately consumed by the next layer, eliminating redundant DRAM round trips. However, DepFiN's mapping is fixed at
architecture-definition time lacking flexibility to
efficiently serve diverse workloads.

To overcome this rigidity, an analytical model for
fused-layer mapping is needed, yet fusing $F$~layers into a unified loop nest increases loop dimensions, introduces strict producer--consumer dependencies between layers, and creates new inter-layer data-reuse opportunities. This proliferation leads to a combinatorial explosion in the design space: for a 10-layer fusion on DepFiN, it expands the map space by over 400 orders of magnitude compared to its single-layer equivalent.
Taming this space requires enforcing cascading
tile-size dependencies between layers to prune infeasible mappings.
Furthermore, since the mapping of a single layer is natively expressed
as a loop nest, it is highly desirable to maintain this
representational consistency across the extended fused-layer map
space. No prior tool represents both single-layer and fused-layer mappings
under a unified, coherent loop-nest abstraction while preserving
heterogeneous per-layer dataflow freedom.

Therefore, our work formally explores the fused-layer dataflow design space through a unified analytical cost mode.
This study formalizes the fused-layer map space,
develops an exact constraint-based pruning strategy,
constructs a per-layer analytical cost model to evaluate performance.
Finally, an extensive design-space exploration across two SAs and four workloads
quantifies the conditions under which layer fusion provides a tangible benefit versus detrimental
overheads.


% ====================================================================
%  §3  PROBLEM FORMULATION
% ====================================================================
\section*{Problem Formulation}
Transitioning from a single-layer execution model to multi-layer fusion is not a simple concatenation of operations; it fundamentally alters the mapping problem, introduces qualitatively new
points to the problem to address and mapping decisions that have no single-layer counterpart and exponentially expands the map space.


\paragraph{Dimension proliferation and Map Space Explosion.}
A single 2-D convolution has $|D| = 6$ loop dimensions.
When $F$ layers are fused into a unified loop nest,
each layer contributes its own set of dimensions.
The total dimension count grows as:

\begingroup
\small
\begin{equation*}
  |D_{\text{fused}}| = 
  \underbrace{4F}_{\substack{\text{per-layer:}\\ C_i, R_i, S_i, Z_i}} + 
  \underbrace{2(F-1)}_{\substack{\text{intermediate}\\ \text{spatial: } Y_i, X_i}} + 
  \underbrace{2}_{\substack{\text{output}\\ P, Q}} 
  = 6F.
\end{equation*}
\endgroup
For the 10-layer FSRCNN fusion (10 fused + 1 output) used in validation, this yields
$|D_{\text{fused}}| = 66$, an order-of-magnitude increase.

The new map-space cardinality is the product of loop permutations at
every memory level and factor allocations across all hierarchy
levels:
%
\begingroup
\small
\begin{equation*}
  |\mathcal{MS}| \;=\;
  \underbrace{(|D|!)^{L_m}}_{\text{loop permutations}}
  \;\cdot\;
  \underbrace{
    \prod_{d \in D}\;\prod_{p \mid d}
      \binom{v_p(d) + L - 1}{L - 1}
  }_{\text{factor allocations}},
  \label{eq:es-mapspace}
\end{equation*}
\endgroup
%
where $L_m$ is the number of memory levels and $L$ the total number
of levels (memory + spatial).


\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Map-space comparison.}
  \label{tab:es-mapspace}
  \small
  \begin{tabularx}{\columnwidth}{X r r}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Quantity}
      & \textbf{Single}
      & \textbf{11-layer} \\
    \midrule
    Dimensions ($|D|$)          & 6     & 66 \\
    Memory levels ($L_m$)       & 5     & 5 \\
    Total levels ($L$)          & 7     & 7 \\
    Permutation term            & $\sim\!10^{11}$
                                & $\sim\!10^{460}$ \\
    Factor-allocation term      & $\sim\!10^{13}$--$10^{20}$
                                & $\sim\!10^{55}$ \\
    \addlinespace
    \textbf{Total map-space}    & $\sim\!10^{24}$--$10^{32}$
                                & $>\!10^{515}$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}
\noindent
The fused map space exceeds $10^{515}$, over 400~orders of magnitude
larger than the single-layer case.

\paragraph{Cascading inter-layer tile-size dependencies.}
Fusion introduces strict producer--consumer constraints.
Selecting a two dimensional output spatial tile of size $P \times Q$ for the final layer mathematically forces every preceding layer to compute a progressively larger tile to account for overlapping filter halo. Accounting for independent strides along the spatial dimensions, this constraint propagates backward according to:

\begin{equation*}
\begin{split}
  &\text{Layer } \text{intermediate footprint:} \\ 
  &\bigl(\text{stride}_h \cdot (P - 1) + R\bigr) \times\; \bigl(\text{stride}_w \cdot (Q - 1) + S\bigr)
\end{split}
\end{equation*}

Eliminating more DRAM traffic but
progressively tighten the on-chip memory constraints for every
participating layer.

This backward propagation dictates that inter-layer tile sizes are \emph{strictly dependent}. A tiling choice made for the outermost layer rigidly constrains the required intermediate tile sizes at every preceding level of the computation. 


A 1-D output layer tile of size $P$ forces every
preceding layer~$i$ to produce a progressively larger tile to account
for the cumulative filter halo:
%
\begin{equation}
  \text{Layer } i: \quad
  \Bigl(P + \textstyle\sum_{j=i+1}^{F-1}(R_j - 1)\Bigr)
\end{equation}
%


\paragraph{The Need for Per-Layer Mapping and Flexible Execution Granularity.} 
A naive strategy for transitioning to multi-layer fusion mapping representation, is mathematically compose the layers by completely merging their loop indices into a monolithic mathematical formula, . However, this over-simplification mathematically eliminates the explicit representation of the intermediate feature maps. This over-simplification introduces sever architectural modeling limitations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Loss of Per-Layer Mapping Freedom:} DNN workloads frequently compose layers with vastly different topologies, dictating different ideal dataflows in the optimal mapping. Forcing all fused layers to share the exact same loop tiling and ordering destroys the ability to express heterogeneous dataflows, as any loop transformation applied to the first layer would rigidly and unavoidably transform the others.
    
    \item \textbf{Back-to-Back Execution:} Collapsing intermediate iteration variables forces the operations into a strict back-to-back scalar schedule. Because no loop variable can advance the producer without advancing the consumer, the producer executes a Multiply-Accumulate (MAC) operation to generate the output that the consumer must immediately use as input, completely eliminating opportunities for temporal buffering.
\end{itemize}

Therefore, a robust formulation of the fused-layer design space must mathematically decouple the iteration spaces of the fused layers. It must explicitly represent intermediate dimensions to allow for distinct, per-layer mapping choices.

%  §4  RELATED WORKS
% ====================================================================
\section*{Related Works}

\paragraph{Layer-by-layer dataflow exploration.}
With the advent of spatial architectures, analytical modeling
frameworks and automated mappers have been developed to navigate
the single-layer map space.
Timeloop introduced a nested-loop
representation of workloads and a mapper--model paradigm that
became a reference point for subsequent work, achieving rapid energy and throughput estimates.
ZigZag unifies tiling and loop ordering by assigning
each prime factor of a workload dimension its own loop, enabling
aggressive pruning heuristics that heavily reduce the search space, at the risk of eliminating optimal solutions.
FactorFlow~\cite{Factorflow} further improves efficiency
by modeling spatial fanout levels explicitly and inverting the
mapper paradigm to achieve
up to $182\times$ faster runtimes for convolution workloads.
Its lightweight evaluation engine makes it the natural baseline for
this study, where the exponential map-space growth of fusion demands
minimal per-evaluation overhead.


\paragraph{Fused-layer dataflow exploration.}
DeFiNES~\cite{mei2023defines} extends the single-layer ZigZag to cross-layer schedules, 
formalizing the depth-first design space along tile size, overlap storing mode, and fuse depth.
However, DeFiNES restricts cross-layer tiling to spatial dimensions, prohibiting channel tiling. By supporting channel tiling, this study explores a richer design space.

LoopTree~\cite{gilbert2024looptree} proposes an extensive design
space and a highly accurate model for fused-layer dataflows,
capturing inter-layer tiling, data retention, and recomputation.
Yet it inherits Timeloop's exhaustive mapping strategies, which
struggle to scale in a fused-layer paradigm.
Furthermore, LoopTree adopts a tree-based representation that
departs from the nested-loop abstraction, whereas our work
maintains consistency with the loop-nest formalism.

At the graph level, Optimus~\cite{10.1145/3461648.3463848} determines \emph{which} layers to fuse and is strictly
complementary to this work.

%  §5  PROPOSED SOLUTION
% ====================================================================
\section*{Proposed Solution}

This study develops an analytical framework for fused-layer mapping
that can represent, prune, and evaluate the multi-layer map space.
The framework builds upon the FactorFlow~\cite{Factorflow}
factor-based abstraction and introduces three modeling concepts and
a generalized per-layer cost model.

\paragraph{1.\ Aliasing: naming the intermediate dimensions.}
When layers are fused, each intermediate activation $A_i$ serves a
dual role: output of layer~$i$ and input of layer~$i\!+\!1$.
Without explicit naming, these shared tensors can only be indexed
through nested affine sums, collapsing the two layers into a rigid
back-to-back scalar schedule with no per-layer mapping freedom.
\emph{Aliasing} resolves this by introducing concrete loop variables
($Y_i, X_i, Z_i$) for each intermediate tensor, registering them in
the coupling so that the producer indexes the tensor through native
dimensions while the consumer indexes it through affine dim-sums
involving its own variables.
The intermediate dimensions become first-class citizens of the
unified loop nest: they can be tiled, reordered, and parallelized
independently.

\paragraph{2.\ Decoupling: per-layer mapping independence.}
Because aliasing assigns syntactically distinct variable names to
each layer, the per-layer dimension sets become disjoint:
$\mathcal{D}_i \cap \mathcal{D}_j = \emptyset$ for $i \neq j$.
This \emph{decoupling} guarantees that iterating over any dimension
of one layer leaves all other layers stationary.
As a direct consequence, the unified dataflow can encode a
\emph{different stationarity} for each layer within a single loop
nest (e.g., weight-stationary for layer~0 and output-stationary for
layer~1).

\paragraph{3.\ Producer--consumer feasibility pruning.}
Aliasing and decoupling give the mapper freedom, but that freedom
introduces a risk: the loop nest no longer guarantees
that every intermediate datum is produced before it is consumed.
To tame the resulting map-space explosion, the framework utilizes a pruning rule to systematically eliminate infeasible mappings before cost evaluation.
The validator checks constraints not at isolated memory levels, but at boundaries defined by consecutive producer dimension instances (zones), which can span multiple memory levels.
It mathematically verifies that the cumulative producer tile size ($X_{cum}$) is strictly large enough to cover the consumer's spatial and channel access patterns ($Q_{zone}$ and $S_{zone}$)
Cascading tile-size inequalities, such as $Q_{zone} + S_{zone} - 1 \le X_{cum}$, are rigorously enforced across width, height, and channel axis.
\begin{align}
  Q_{\text{zone}} + S_{\text{zone}} - 1 &\;\le\; X_{\text{cum}},
    \tag{Width} \\[2pt]
  P_{\text{zone}} + R_{\text{zone}} - 1 &\;\le\; Y_{\text{cum}},
    \tag{Height} \\[2pt]
  C_{i+1,\,\text{zone}} &\;\le\; Z_{i,\,\text{cum}}.
    \tag{Channel}
\end{align}
Every candidate mapping move triggers this validator; moves that
violate any constraint are rolled back immediately.
The pruning is \emph{exact}: it removes only physically infeasible
mappings, preserving every valid candidate.

For instance, consider a width-axis configuration with full extents
$X = 18$, $Q = 16$, $S = 3$ on a three-level hierarchy
(DRAM, Global Buffer, Register).
Here subscripts denote the \emph{hierarchy level}, not the layer
index.
A candidate mapping tiles these dimensions as shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:es-pruning-zones} (from outermost to innermost):
$Q_1\!=\!4$, $X_1\!=\!3$, $S_1\!=\!1$, $Q_2\!=\!4$, $X_2\!=\!2$,
$S_2\!=\!1$, $Q_3\!=\!1$, $S_3\!=\!3$, $X_3\!=\!3$.
The three $X$ loops partition the nest into three \emph{zones};

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \label{tab:es-pruning-zones}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{l c c c l}

\paragraph{4.\ Per-layer cost model.}
The single-layer cost model is generalized to compute memory
operations, energy, and latency on a per-layer basis.
Reads and writes are attributed to each layer through per-layer
dimension filtering, and operand bypass rules are extended to the
five fused categories.
The energy is decomposed as:
%
%
\begingroup
\small
\begin{equation*}
  \text{WMOPs}_i \;=\;
    \sum_{l \in \text{levels}}
      e_l \cdot \bigl(R_l^{(i)} + W_l^{(i)}\bigr),
\end{equation*}
\endgroup
%
where $e_l$ is the per-access energy at level~$l$.
The latency model detects bandwidth-limited stalls per layer per
level:
%
%
\begingroup
\small
\begin{equation*}
  \Lambda_l^{(i)} \;=\;
    \max\!\bigl(
      \Lambda_{l,\text{rd}}^{(i)},\;
      \Lambda_{l,\text{wr}}^{(i)}
    \bigr),
  \qquad
  \Lambda \;=\;
    \max_{l}\;\sum_{i=0}^{F-1} \Lambda_l^{(i)}.
\end{equation*}
\endgroup
%
The energy--delay product provides a single-number
efficiency metric that captures the trade-off between energy and
throughput.

\section*{Validation}

The framework is validated against DepFiN~\cite{goetschalckx2023depfin}, a 12\,nm depth-first
CNN chip with a $16 \times 128$ PE array, 1056\,KB Feature Memory,
and 524\,KB Weight Memory.
The validation reproduces the execution of an 11-layer CNN workload 
(77.7\,billion MACs at 720$\times$1280 resolution):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Ideal latency}: the model predicts 37.94\,M cycles,
        matching the compute-bound reference
        ($\text{Total MACs} / \text{PEs}$) exactly.
  \item \textbf{DRAM bypass}: zero intermediate DRAM traffic,
        reproducing DepFiN's depth-first fusion guarantee.
  \item \textbf{On-chip stalls and feature shifter}: the model explicitly captures the hardware's feature shift register, demonstrating how intra-PE temporal reuse reduces the required Feature Memory (FMEM) bandwidth. The analytical engine reveals that without this mechanism, a standard FMEM interface would trigger memory-bound stalls, causing a 10\% latency overhead. With the shifter active, at regime execution achieves absolutely zero stalls.
  \item \textbf{Weight Memory}: zero stall cycles, confirming
        sufficient bandwidth for weight delivery.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
These results establish that the analytical model faithfully captures
the data-movement behavior and timing of a real fused-layer
accelerator.


% ====================================================================
%  §7  Experiments
% ====================================================================
\section*{Experiments}

The framework is exercised on a systematic design-space
exploration covering two spatial architectures (DepFiN-like and Eyeriss-v1-like), four CNN workloads (FSRCNN, MC-CNN --activation-dominant;
VGG16, ResNet18 --weight-dominant), three execution modes (full
fusion, partial fusion, single-layer), and two architecture-sizing
scenarios (Scenario~A sized for full fusion, Scenario~B sized for
partial fusion).

\paragraph{DRAM traffic elimination.}
Full fusion reduces total DRAM traffic by $94.8\%$ (FSRCNN),
$85.3\%$ (MC-CNN), $59.9\%$ (VGG16), and $25.1\%$ (ResNet18),
confirming that fusion eliminates the dominant off-chip transfers
for activation-heavy workloads.

\paragraph{Latency.}
Fusion delivers $42$--$48\%$ latency savings for activation-dominant
workloads on the DepFiN-like architecture and $16$--$46\%$ on
Eyeriss.
For weight-dominant workloads, the outcome is architecture-dependent:
Eyeriss still reduces latency ($7.8$--$21.2\%$) because its
distributed per-PE registers avoid a single shared-memory contention
point, while DepFiN can be \emph{slower} under full fusion (up to
$30.3\%$ latency increase for ResNet18) because the very large
shared weight memory (10.7--14.4\,MB) becomes a bandwidth
bottleneck.
Partial (pairwise) fusion captures $87$--$103\%$ of the full-fusion
latency benefit, indicating that nearly all the inter-layer saving
is realized by fusing just two consecutive layers.

\paragraph{Energy: workload-dependent and architecture-dependent.}
The energy outcome depends critically on the interplay between the
DRAM cost saved and the on-chip memory overhead incurred, and is
consequently sensitive to the DRAM per-access energy.
At the tried DRAM per-access energy of 160\,pJ\,/\,byte, full fusion
is \emph{energy-beneficial} on DepFiN for activation-dominant
workloads: FSRCNN single-layer energy is $4.38\times$ the
full-fusion value, and MC-CNN is $1.73\times$, because the
eliminated DRAM traffic is massive and the feature-memory overhead
modest.
While for weight-dominant workloads on DepFiN, the picture reverses:
VGG16 single-layer energy is only $7.4\%$ of the full-fusion value,
and ResNet18 is $17.3\%$, because 10--14\,MB weight memories inflate
per-access energy far beyond the DRAM savings.
On Eyeriss, the replicated per-PE register architecture amplifies
the on-chip overhead: fusion is roughly energy-neutral for FSRCNN
and energy-adverse for all other workloads.

\paragraph{Energy--Delay Product.}
Full fusion wins on EDP in three of the eight architecture--workload
combinations: FSRCNN on DepFiN ($6.44\times$ single/full), MC-CNN
on DepFiN ($2.31\times$), and FSRCNN on Eyeriss ($1.75\times$).
In all three cases, the activation-dominant nature of the workload
ensures that both energy and latency favor fusion, amplifying the
EDP advantage.
For the remaining five combinations, single-layer execution is the
more efficient choice.

\paragraph{Partial fusion as a practical compromise.}
Sizing the architecture for pairwise rather than full fusion
(Scenario~B) reduced the weight memory by up to $3.1\times$ on
DepFiN and the per-PE register by up to $2.1\times$ on Eyeriss,
while preserving $96$--$100\%$ of the full-fusion latency saving
for activation-dominant workloads.
For activation-dominant workloads on DepFiN, partial fusion won on
energy, latency, and EDP simultaneously, making it the strictly
dominant strategy.

\paragraph{Super-linear scaling of the energy penalty.}
The fusion-to-single energy ratio is not a fixed constant in different PE configurations of Eyeriss:
each $2\times$ increase in per-PE register size raises the ratio by
$1.5$--$1.9\times$ (ResNet18: $8.0\times \to 11.7\times \to
19.6\times$; VGG16: $17.0\times \to 28.9\times \to 54.1\times$).
A designer who shrinks the PE array to save area will face a
proportionally larger register and a correspondingly worse energy
ratio.

\section*{Conclusions}

The central theoretical contribution of this thesis is demonstrating
that the fused-layer map space, despite its astronomical size
($>10^{515}$ configurations for a 10-layer fusion), can be rigorously
formalized, navigated, and evaluated within a single, unified loop-nest
abstraction.
The key enablers are the \emph{aliasing} and \emph{decoupling}
mechanisms.
Aliasing introduces concrete loop variables for every intermediate
activation, whose dimensions can be tiled, reordered, and
parallelized independently.
Decoupling follows directly: the per-layer dimension
sets become disjoint,
so iterating over any dimension of one layer
leaves all others stationary.
This property guarantees that the unified loop nest can encode a
\emph{different dataflow} for each fused layer, for instance,
weight-stationary for one layer and output-stationary for the
next, without requiring separate per-layer scheduling passes.
No prior fused-layer framework offers this combination of a coherent
loop-nest representation with full per-layer mapping independence.
Combined with exact producer--consumer feasibility pruning, the
framework eliminates only physically infeasible mappings while
preserving every valid candidate, and the per-layer analytical cost
model accurately reproduces the behavior of the DepFiN accelerator.

The experiments clarify the fundamental trade-offs of layer fusion.
Layer fusion is a \emph{workload-dependent and
architecture-dependent optimization} whose energy outcome is
sensitive to the DRAM technology cost.
At 160\,pJ/byte DRAM access energy, fusion is energy-beneficial for
activation-dominant workloads on shared-memory architectures,
yielding EDP improvements of up to $6.4\times$.
For weight-dominant workloads, the on-chip memory overhead dominates,
and single-layer execution remains more efficient.
Partial (pairwise) fusion emerges as the most practical deployment
strategy: it captures nearly all the latency benefit of full fusion
at a fraction of the memory cost and, for activation-dominant
workloads on DepFiN, is the strictly dominant choice on energy,
latency, and EDP simultaneously.

The primary directions for future work include developing automated
heuristic mappers capable of navigating the pruned fused map space,
supporting inter-layer pipeline parallelism, and porting the
analytical engine to a compiled language to enable large-scale
exploration.
