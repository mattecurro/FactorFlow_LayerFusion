\section*{Introduction}

Deep neural networks (DNNs) have become the dominant computational
paradigm across a wide range of applications, from image classification
and object detection to natural language processing and autonomous
driving~\cite{lecun2015deep}.
The computational cost of modern DNNs is immense: a single inference
pass of a large convolutional network such as VGG16 requires billions
of multiply-and-accumulate (MAC) operations~\cite{vgg16}.
While general-purpose processors and GPUs deliver high throughput,
their rigid memory hierarchies often struggle to meet the stringent
energy-efficiency requirements of edge devices.
This bottleneck has motivated the development of dedicated
\emph{spatial architectures} (SAs): custom accelerators that exploit
massive data-level parallelism through arrays of processing elements
(PEs) interconnected by customized on-chip memory networks.
The performance and energy efficiency of such accelerators depend not
only on their hardware design but, critically, on the \emph{mapping}:
a schedule that determines how loop iterations are tiled, ordered,
and distributed across PEs and memory levels.
Even for a single convolutional layer on a moderately complex SA, the
resulting set of all valid mappings, the \emph{map space}, exceeds
$10^{19}$ candidates~\cite{Factorflow}, driving the development of
analytical cost models and automated mappers to search for optimal
schedules.

Despite the possibility of selecting the optimal mapping for each
individual layer, the classic \emph{layer-by-layer} execution model
introduces a fundamental constraint: after one layer completes, its
output activation tensor is written to off-chip DRAM, only to be read
back when the next layer begins.
Because DRAM accesses are roughly two orders of magnitude more
expensive than on-chip SRAM accesses in terms of energy, this
intermediate-activation traffic typically dominates the total energy
budget for activation-heavy workloads and frequently creates
bandwidth-limited processing stalls that severely degrade latency.
\emph{Layer fusion} has emerged as a promising strategy to address
this bottleneck by computing multiple consecutive layers in a single,
interleaved schedule: intermediate activations are produced and
consumed entirely within on-chip buffers in localized chunks,
effectively eliminating the round trip through external DRAM.
Hardware implementations such as DepFiN~\cite{goetschalckx2023depfin}
have demonstrated that depth-first fused execution can achieve
significant reductions in off-chip data movement.

However, reasoning about fused-layer mappings is fundamentally more
complex than the single-layer case.
Fusing $F$~layers into a unified loop nest exponentially multiplies
the number of loop dimensions, introduces strict producer--consumer
dependencies between layers, and creates new inter-layer data-reuse
opportunities.
This dimensional proliferation causes an astronomical growth: for
instance, an 11-layer fusion of FSRCNN on the DepFiN architecture
expands the map space to over $10^{515}$ candidates, more than
400~orders of magnitude larger than its single-layer
equivalent~\cite{Factorflow}.
Taming this intractable space requires studying the cascading
tile-size dependencies between producer and consumer layers to
rigorously prune physically infeasible mappings, analyzing the
different types of buffered and immediate data-reuse mechanisms, and
reasoning about five distinct operand categories (as opposed to three
in the single-layer case).
Furthermore, since the mapping of a single layer is natively expressed
as a loop nest, it is highly desirable to maintain this
representational consistency across the extended fused-layer map
space.
Existing analytical frameworks either operate on a single layer at a
time~\cite{parashar2019timeloop, Factorflow} or, when they do address
fusion, constrain the admissible dataflows or lack the generality to
represent per-layer mapping independence within a unified loop
nest~\cite{mei2023defines, gilbert2024looptree}.
No prior tool represents both single-layer and fused-layer mappings
under a unified, coherent loop-nest abstraction while preserving
heterogeneous per-layer dataflow freedom.

Therefore, the core contribution of this thesis is a formal analytical
framework for the fused-layer dataflow design space.
The framework formalizes the fused-layer map space,
develops an exact constraint-based pruning strategy that enforces
producer--consumer tile-size feasibility at every hierarchy level,
constructs a per-layer analytical cost model that evaluates memory
operations, energy, and latency for the unified fused loop nest, and
validates the model against a published silicon baseline (DepFiN).
Finally, a comprehensive design-space exploration across two spatial
architectures (DepFiN-Like and Eyeriss-Like) and four CNN workloads
quantifies the architectural and algorithmic conditions under which
layer fusion provides a tangible benefit versus a detrimental
overhead.


% ====================================================================
%  §3  PROBLEM FORMULATION
% ====================================================================
\section*{Problem Formulation}

\paragraph{Dimension proliferation.}
A single 2-D convolution has $|D| = 6$ loop dimensions.
When $F$ consecutive layers are fused into a unified loop nest,
each layer contributes its own set of dimensions.
The total dimension count grows as:
%
\begin{equation}
  |D_{\text{fused}}| \;=\;
  \underbrace{4F}_{\substack{\text{per-layer:}\\
    C_i, R_i, S_i, Z_i}} \;+\;
  \underbrace{2(F-1)}_{\substack{\text{intermediate}\\
    \text{spatial: } Y_i, X_i}} \;+\;
  \underbrace{2}_{\substack{\text{output}\\
    P, Q}}
  \;=\; 6F.
  \tag{Dimension growth}
\end{equation}
%
For the 11-layer FSRCNN fusion used in validation, this yields
$|D_{\text{fused}}| = 66$, an order-of-magnitude increase.

\paragraph{Map-space explosion.}
The map-space cardinality is the product of loop permutations at
every memory level and factor allocations across all hierarchy
levels:
%
\begin{equation}
  |\mathcal{MS}| \;=\;
  \underbrace{(|D|!)^{L_m}}_{\text{loop permutations}}
  \;\cdot\;
  \underbrace{
    \prod_{d \in D}\;\prod_{p \mid d}
      \binom{v_p(d) + L - 1}{L - 1}
  }_{\text{factor allocations}},
  \label{eq:es-mapspace}
\end{equation}
%
where $L_m$ is the number of memory levels and $L$ the total number
of levels (memory + spatial).
Table~\ref{tab:es-mapspace} quantifies the growth.

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \caption{Map-space comparison: single layer vs.\ 11-layer fusion
           on a representative spatial architecture ($L_m = 5$
           memory levels, $L = 7$ total levels).}
  \label{tab:es-mapspace}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{l r r}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Quantity}
      & \textbf{Single layer}
      & \textbf{11-layer fusion} \\
    \midrule
    Dimensions ($|D|$)          & 6     & 66 \\
    Memory levels ($L_m$)       & 5     & 5 \\
    Total levels ($L$)          & 7     & 7 \\
    Permutation term            & $\sim\!10^{11}$
                                & $\sim\!10^{460}$ \\
    Factor-allocation term      & $\sim\!10^{13}$--$10^{20}$
                                & $\sim\!10^{55}$ \\
    \addlinespace
    \textbf{Total map-space}    & $\sim\!10^{24}$--$10^{32}$
                                & $>\!10^{515}$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\noindent
The fused map space exceeds $10^{515}$, over 400~orders of magnitude
larger than the single-layer case.
Unguided exploration has effectively zero probability of discovering
valid, high-quality mappings.

\paragraph{Cascading inter-layer tile-size dependencies.}
Fusion introduces strict producer--consumer constraints.
An output tile of size $P \times Q$ for the last layer forces every
preceding layer~$i$ to produce a progressively larger tile to account
for the cumulative filter halo:
%
\begin{equation}
  \text{Layer } i: \quad
  \Bigl(P + \textstyle\sum_{j=i+1}^{F-1}(R_j - 1)\Bigr)
  \;\times\;
  \Bigl(Q + \textstyle\sum_{j=i+1}^{F-1}(S_j - 1)\Bigr).
  \tag{Cascading tiles}
\end{equation}
%
Deeper fusion topologies eliminate more DRAM traffic but
progressively tighten the on-chip memory constraints for every
participating layer.

\paragraph{Five operand categories.}
Unlike single-layer mapping, which distinguishes three operand types
(input, weights, output), fused mapping requires five categories:
(1)~global input, (2)~per-layer weights $W_0, \dots, W_{F-1}$,
(3)~intermediate output $A_i$, (4)~intermediate input $A_{i-1}$,
and (5)~global output.
Each memory level must specify bypass rules for all five categories;
these rules govern whether intermediate activations stay on-chip,
thereby determining whether the depth-first fusion guarantee is
satisfied.

%  §4  PROPOSED SOLUTION
% ====================================================================
\section*{Proposed Solution}

This thesis develops an analytical framework for fused-layer mapping
that can represent, prune, and evaluate the multi-layer map space.
The framework builds upon the FactorFlow~\cite{Factorflow}
factor-based abstraction and introduces three modeling concepts and
a generalized per-layer cost model.

\paragraph{1.\ Aliasing: naming the intermediate dimensions.}
When layers are fused, each intermediate activation $A_i$ serves a
dual role: output of layer~$i$ and input of layer~$i\!+\!1$.
Without explicit naming, these shared tensors can only be indexed
through nested affine sums, collapsing the two layers into a rigid
back-to-back scalar schedule with no per-layer mapping freedom.
\emph{Aliasing} resolves this by introducing concrete loop variables
($Y_i, X_i, Z_i$) for each intermediate tensor, registering them in
the coupling so that the producer indexes the tensor through native
dimensions while the consumer indexes it through affine dim-sums
involving its own variables.
The intermediate dimensions become first-class citizens of the
unified loop nest: they can be tiled, reordered, and parallelized
independently.

\paragraph{2.\ Decoupling: per-layer mapping independence.}
Because aliasing assigns syntactically distinct variable names to
each layer, the per-layer dimension sets become disjoint:
$\mathcal{D}_i \cap \mathcal{D}_j = \emptyset$ for $i \neq j$.
This \emph{decoupling} guarantees that iterating over any dimension
of one layer leaves all other layers stationary.
As a direct consequence, the unified dataflow can encode a
\emph{different stationarity} for each layer within a single loop
nest (e.g., weight-stationary for layer~0 and output-stationary for
layer~1), without requiring separate per-layer scheduling.

\paragraph{3.\ Producer--consumer feasibility pruning.}
Aliasing and decoupling give the mapper freedom, but that freedom
introduces a risk: the loop nest no longer automatically guarantees
that every intermediate datum is produced before it is consumed.
The framework enforces exact feasibility constraints at every
hierarchy level through cascading tile-size inequalities:
%
\begin{align}
  Q_{\text{zone}} + S_{\text{zone}} - 1 &\;\le\; X_{\text{cum}},
    \tag{Width} \\[2pt]
  P_{\text{zone}} + R_{\text{zone}} - 1 &\;\le\; Y_{\text{cum}},
    \tag{Height} \\[2pt]
  C_{i+1,\,\text{zone}} &\;\le\; Z_{i,\,\text{cum}}.
    \tag{Channel}
\end{align}
%
Every candidate mapping move triggers this validator; moves that
violate any constraint are rolled back immediately.
The pruning is \emph{exact}: it removes only physically infeasible
mappings, preserving every valid candidate.

\paragraph{4.\ Per-layer cost model.}
The single-layer cost model is generalized to compute memory
operations, energy, and latency on a per-layer basis.
Reads and writes are attributed to each layer through per-layer
dimension filtering, and operand bypass rules are extended to the
five fused categories.
The energy is decomposed as:
%
\begin{equation}
  \text{WMOPs}_i \;=\;
    \sum_{l \in \text{levels}}
      e_l \cdot \bigl(R_l^{(i)} + W_l^{(i)}\bigr),
  \tag{Per-layer energy}
\end{equation}
%
where $e_l$ is the per-access energy at level~$l$.
The latency model detects bandwidth-limited stalls per layer per
level:
%
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda_l^{(i)} \;=\;
    \max\!\bigl(
      \Lambda_{l,\text{rd}}^{(i)},\;
      \Lambda_{l,\text{wr}}^{(i)}
    \bigr),
  \qquad
  \Lambda \;=\;
    \max_{l}\;\sum_{i=0}^{F-1} \Lambda_l^{(i)}.
  \tag{Latency}
\end{equation}
%
The energy--delay product
$\text{EDP} = \text{WMOPs} \times \Lambda$ provides a single-number
efficiency metric that captures the trade-off between energy and
throughput.

\section*{Validation}

The framework is validated against the DepFiN
accelerator~\cite{goetschalckx2023depfin}, a 22\,nm depth-first
CNN chip with a $16 \times 128$ PE array, 1056\,KB Feature Memory,
and 524\,KB Weight Memory.
The validation reproduces the execution of an 11-layer FSRCNN
workload (77.7\,billion MACs at 720$\times$1280 resolution):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Ideal latency}: the model predicts 37.94\,M cycles,
        matching the compute-bound reference
        ($\text{Total MACs} / \text{PEs}$) exactly.
  \item \textbf{DRAM bypass}: zero intermediate DRAM traffic,
        reproducing DepFiN's depth-first fusion guarantee.
  \item \textbf{On-chip stalls}: only 24 stall cycles out of
        37.94\,M arise from the Feature Memory, exclusively at the
        boundary layers (L0 and L10).
        At regime (L1--L9), zero stall cycles.
  \item \textbf{Weight Memory}: zero stall cycles, confirming
        sufficient bandwidth for weight delivery.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent
These results establish that the analytical model faithfully captures
the data-movement behavior and timing of a real fused-layer
accelerator.

\section*{Key Findings}

The validated framework is exercised on a systematic design-space
exploration covering two spatial architectures (DepFiN-like with
shared on-chip memories, Eyeriss-v1-like with replicated per-PE
registers), four CNN workloads (FSRCNN, MC-CNN---activation-dominant;
VGG16, ResNet18---weight-dominant), three execution modes (full
fusion, partial fusion, single-layer), and two architecture-sizing
scenarios (Scenario~A sized for full fusion, Scenario~B sized for
pairwise fusion).

\paragraph{DRAM traffic elimination.}
Full fusion reduces total DRAM traffic by $94.8\%$ (FSRCNN),
$85.3\%$ (MC-CNN), $59.9\%$ (VGG16), and $25.1\%$ (ResNet18),
confirming that fusion eliminates the dominant off-chip transfers
for activation-heavy workloads.

\paragraph{Latency.}
Fusion delivers $42$--$48\%$ latency savings for activation-dominant
workloads on the DepFiN-like architecture and $16$--$46\%$ on
Eyeriss.
For weight-dominant workloads, the outcome is architecture-dependent:
Eyeriss still reduces latency ($7.8$--$21.2\%$) because its
distributed per-PE registers avoid a single shared-memory contention
point, while DepFiN can be \emph{slower} under full fusion (up to
$30.3\%$ latency increase for ResNet18) because the very large
shared weight memory (10.7--14.4\,MB) becomes a bandwidth
bottleneck.
Partial (pairwise) fusion captures $87$--$103\%$ of the full-fusion
latency benefit, indicating that nearly all the inter-layer saving
is realized by fusing just two consecutive layers.

\paragraph{Energy: workload-dependent and architecture-dependent.}
The energy outcome depends critically on the interplay between the
DRAM cost saved and the on-chip memory overhead incurred, and is
consequently sensitive to the DRAM per-access energy.
At 160\,pJ\,/\,byte (Section~\ref{sec:energy-model}), full fusion
is \emph{energy-beneficial} on DepFiN for activation-dominant
workloads: FSRCNN single-layer energy is $4.38\times$ the
full-fusion value, and MC-CNN is $1.73\times$, because the
eliminated DRAM traffic is massive and the feature-memory overhead
modest.
For weight-dominant workloads on DepFiN, the picture reverses:
VGG16 single-layer energy is only $7.4\%$ of the full-fusion value,
and ResNet18 is $17.3\%$, because 10--14\,MB weight memories inflate
per-access energy far beyond the DRAM savings.
On Eyeriss, the replicated per-PE register architecture amplifies
the on-chip overhead: fusion is roughly energy-neutral for FSRCNN
and energy-adverse for all other workloads.

\paragraph{Energy--Delay Product.}
Full fusion wins on EDP in three of the eight architecture--workload
combinations: FSRCNN on DepFiN ($6.44\times$ single/full), MC-CNN
on DepFiN ($2.31\times$), and FSRCNN on Eyeriss ($1.75\times$).
In all three cases, the activation-dominant nature of the workload
ensures that both energy and latency favor fusion, amplifying the
EDP advantage.
For the remaining five combinations, single-layer execution is the
more efficient choice.

\paragraph{Partial fusion as a practical compromise.}
Sizing the architecture for pairwise rather than full fusion
(Scenario~B) reduced the weight memory by up to $3.1\times$ on
DepFiN and the per-PE register by up to $2.1\times$ on Eyeriss,
while preserving $96$--$100\%$ of the full-fusion latency saving
for activation-dominant workloads.
For activation-dominant workloads on DepFiN, partial fusion won on
energy, latency, and EDP simultaneously, making it the strictly
dominant strategy.

\paragraph{Super-linear scaling of the energy penalty.}
The fusion-to-single energy ratio is not a fixed constant: each
$2\times$ increase in per-PE register size raises the ratio by
$1.5$--$1.9\times$ (ResNet18: $8.0\times \to 11.7\times \to
19.6\times$; VGG16: $17.0\times \to 28.9\times \to 54.1\times$).
A designer who shrinks the PE array to save area will face a
proportionally larger register and a correspondingly worse energy
ratio.

%  §7  CONCLUSIONS

\section*{Conclusions}

This thesis demonstrates that the fused-layer map space, despite its
astronomical size ($>10^{515}$ configurations for an 11-layer
fusion), can be rigorously formalized, pruned, and evaluated through
the aliasing, decoupling, and constraint-based feasibility framework
developed here.
The per-layer analytical cost model accurately reproduces the
behavior of the DepFiN accelerator, and the systematic experimental
campaign clarifies the fundamental trade-offs of layer fusion.

The central finding is that layer fusion is a \emph{workload-dependent
and architecture-dependent optimization} whose energy outcome is
sensitive to the DRAM technology cost.
At 160\,pJ\,/\,byte, fusion is energy-beneficial for
activation-dominant workloads on shared-memory architectures,
yielding EDP improvements of up to $6.4\times$.
For weight-dominant workloads, the on-chip memory overhead dominates,
and single-layer execution remains more efficient.
Partial (pairwise) fusion emerges as the most practical deployment
strategy: it captures nearly all the latency benefit of full fusion
at a fraction of the memory cost and, for activation-dominant
workloads on DepFiN, is the strictly dominant choice on energy,
latency, and EDP simultaneously.

The primary directions for future work include developing automated
heuristic mappers capable of navigating the pruned fused map space,
supporting inter-layer pipeline parallelism, and porting the
analytical engine to a compiled language to enable large-scale
exploration.
