\section{Context}
\label{sec:intro-context}

Deep neural networks (DNNs) have become the dominant computational
paradigm across a wide range of applications, from image classification
and object detection to natural language processing and autonomous
driving~\cite{lecun2015deep}.
The computational cost of modern DNNs, however, is immense: a single
inference pass of a large convolutional network such as VGG16 requires
billions of multiply-and-accumulate (MAC) operations, while training
demands orders of magnitude more~\cite{vgg_paper}.
General-purpose processors---CPUs and GPUs---struggle to deliver the
energy efficiency and throughput required by edge and data-centre
deployments, motivating the development of dedicated \emph{spatial
architectures} (SAs): custom accelerators that exploit massive
data-level parallelism through arrays of processing elements (PEs)
interconnected by an on-chip network~\cite{sze2017efficient}.

The performance and energy efficiency of a spatial architecture depend
not only on its hardware design but, critically, on the \emph{mapping}:
a schedule that determines the order in which loop iterations are
executed, how data are tiled and distributed across PEs, and which
operands are retained in each level of the memory hierarchy.
For a single convolutional layer on a moderately complex SA, the
resulting \emph{map space}---the set of all valid mappings---already
exceeds $10^{19}$ candidates~\cite{Factorflow}, making exhaustive
search intractable and motivating the development of analytical cost
models and automated mappers.

Several frameworks have been proposed to address this challenge.
\textbf{Timeloop}~\cite{parashar2019timeloop} introduced the
mapper--model paradigm, pairing a search engine with an analytical
cost model that estimates energy, latency, and data movement for
each candidate mapping.
\textbf{FactorFlow}~\cite{Factorflow} reformulated the problem
by adopting the extended Einsum notation~\cite{een} and treating
the map space as a factor-manipulation problem, enabling a broader
class of mappers---linear, quadratic, and hybrid---to navigate the
space more efficiently.

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:intro-motivation}

Despite their effectiveness, both Timeloop and FactorFlow operate
on a \emph{single layer at a time}.
In a layer-by-layer execution model, every intermediate activation
tensor is written to off-chip DRAM after one layer completes and
read back when the next layer begins.
Because DRAM accesses are roughly two orders of magnitude more
expensive than on-chip SRAM accesses in terms of energy, this
ping-pong pattern dominates the total energy budget for many
workloads~\cite{sze2017efficient}.

\emph{Layer fusion} addresses this bottleneck by computing
multiple consecutive layers in a single, interleaved schedule.
Intermediate activations are produced and consumed entirely
within on-chip buffers, eliminating the round trip through DRAM.
Hardware implementations such as DepFiN~\cite{goetschalckx2023depfin}
have demonstrated that depth-first, fused execution can achieve
significant energy savings for activation-dominant workloads.

However, reasoning about fused-layer mappings is far more complex
than the single-layer case.
Fusing $L$~layers multiplies the number of loop dimensions,
introduces cascading tile-size dependencies between producers
and consumers, creates five distinct operand categories
(input activations, output activations, weights,
intermediate-input, and intermediate-output), and
causes the map space to grow to astronomical proportions.
For an 8-layer fusion of FSRCNN on DepFiN, the map space
exceeds $10^{463}$~candidates---over 400~orders of magnitude
larger than the single-layer space~\cite{Factorflow}.
Existing fused-layer exploration frameworks, such as
DeFiNES~\cite{DeFiNES} and LoopTree~\cite{LoopTree}, make
progress on this front but typically impose constraints on the
schedule structure or the set of admissible dataflows.
No prior tool extends the full generality of the FactorFlow
factor-manipulation paradigm to multi-layer fusion.

\section{Objectives}
\label{sec:intro-objectives}

The primary objective of this thesis is to extend FactorFlow with
support for fused-layer mappings, producing a tool that can:
%
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Represent} fused-layer computations within the
        existing extended Einsum and factor-based framework, using
        aliasing and decoupling to unify multi-layer loop nests
        without sacrificing per-layer independence.

  \item \textbf{Navigate} the combinatorial fused-layer map space
        through a depth-first scheduling heuristic that prunes
        infeasible mappings by enforcing the producer--consumer
        tile-size inequality at every hierarchy level.

  \item \textbf{Evaluate} candidate fused-layer mappings with an
        analytical cost model that accounts for per-layer memory
        operations, energy (via Accelergy~\cite{accelergy}), and
        latency---including bandwidth-limited stall cycles.

  \item \textbf{Validate} the extended model against a published
        hardware baseline (DepFiN), demonstrating that the
        analytical predictions match the reference design within
        an acceptable error margin.

  \item \textbf{Explore} the design space by conducting sensitivity
        analyses on two spatial architectures (DepFiN and Eyeriss~v1)
        and four CNN workloads, comparing full fusion, partial fusion,
        and single-layer execution modes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:intro-contributions}

The main contributions of this work are:
%
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Formal representation of fused-layer mappings.}
        A representation scheme based on \emph{aliasing} of
        shared activation dimensions and \emph{decoupling} of
        per-layer weight dimensions, which integrates multi-layer
        fusion into the FactorFlow factor-based abstraction without
        altering its single-layer semantics
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:proposed-solution},
         Section~\ref{sec:ps-modeling}).

  \item \textbf{Depth-first scheduling heuristic.}
        A validity heuristic that enforces the cascading
        tile-size inequality at every hierarchy level,
        enabling the mapper to prune infeasible regions
        of the map space before cost evaluation
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:proposed-solution},
         Section~\ref{sec:ps-heuristic}).

  \item \textbf{Per-layer cost-model extensions.}
        Generalisation of the single-layer cost model to
        compute memory operations, energy, and latency
        on a per-layer basis, including a stall-cycle model
        that detects bandwidth-limited memory levels
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:proposed-solution},
         Section~\ref{sec:ps-cost-model}).

  \item \textbf{Tool implementation.}
        A complete Python implementation extending the FactorFlow
        codebase, comprising workload, architecture, and mapping
        specification modules, a cost-model engine, and a set
        of mappers (linear, quadratic, hybrid)
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:proposed-solution},
         Section~\ref{sec:ps-implementation}).

  \item \textbf{Validation against DepFiN.}
        A validation study reproducing the depth-first execution
        of an 11-layer FSRCNN workload on the DepFiN accelerator,
        comparing model predictions against published silicon data
        (Chapter~\ref{ch:validation}).

  \item \textbf{Experimental design-space exploration.}
        A comprehensive set of experiments covering two
        architectures (DepFiN-like and Eyeriss-v1-like),
        four CNN workloads (FSRCNN, MC-CNN, VGG16, ResNet18),
        and three fusion modes (full, partial, single-layer),
        quantifying the energy, latency, and
        energy-delay product trade-offs of layer fusion.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Thesis Organisation}
\label{sec:intro-organisation}

The remainder of this thesis is organised as follows.

\begin{description}
  \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:background} --- Background.]
    Introduces the foundational concepts: artificial intelligence
    and deep learning, DNN workloads and the extended Einsum notation,
    hardware acceleration with spatial architectures, the mapping
    problem and map-space explosion, analytical cost modelling,
    and layer fusion.

  \item[Chapter~3 --- Related Works.]
    Reviews the state of the art in layer-by-layer dataflow
    exploration (Timeloop, FactorFlow), fused-layer frameworks
    (DeFiNES, LoopTree), hardware implementations (ISAAC, DepFiN),
    and graph-level optimisation tools (Optimus).

  \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:fused-design-space} --- Fused-Layer
    Mapping Design Space.]
    Characterises the fused-layer mapping problem: the
    proliferation of dimensions, cascading tile dependencies,
    the five operand categories, data-reuse strategies
    (immediate, buffered, fully cached), and the combinatorial
    growth of the map space.

  \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:proposed-solution} --- Proposed Solution.]
    Presents the FactorFlow extension: the aliasing and decoupling
    model, the depth-first scheduling heuristic, cost-model
    generalisations, and implementation details.

  \item[Chapter~\ref{ch:validation} --- Validation.]
    Validates the extended model against the DepFiN accelerator
    executing an 11-layer FSRCNN workload, analysing the
    agreement between predicted and published data-movement
    and energy figures.

  \item[Chapter~7 --- Experimental Results.]
    Reports the design-space exploration: experimental setup
    (architectures, workloads, metrics), single-architecture
    sensitivity analyses (tile size, PE count, register sizing),
    and the layer-fusion comparison across full, partial, and
    single-layer execution modes.

  \item[Chapter~8 --- Conclusions and Future Work.]
    Summarises the findings and outlines directions for
    future research, including support for residual connections,
    automated architecture sizing, and integration with
    graph-level scheduling.
\end{description}
