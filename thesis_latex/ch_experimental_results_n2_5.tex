\subsection{Eyeriss: Intermediate and Output Register Sweeps (CS2, CS3)}
\label{sec:eyeriss-cs2-cs3}

\subsubsection{Motivation}

The previous section showed that the weight register is the dominant
sizing constraint for the Eyeriss-Like architecture under full fusion.
Two additional per-PE registers, however, also contribute to the
overall register pressure: the \emph{intermediate register} (IntReg),
which stores intermediate activations passed between consecutive fused
layers, and the \emph{output register} (OutReg), which accumulates the
partial sums of the final fused layer before they are written back to
the GlobalBuffer.

In principle, each register can become the binding constraint if it is
undersized relative to the per-PE footprint imposed by the spatial
mapping.
A register is \emph{binding} when its required footprint exceeds the
allocated capacity, forcing the PE array to grow (more columns) so
that the spatial distribution reduces the per-PE share.
A register is \emph{non-binding} when the footprint already fits
comfortably, and enlarging it has no effect on the minimum feasible PE
count.

CS2 and CS3 sweep IntReg and OutReg independently while holding all
other parameters constant (GlobalBuffer\,=\,128\,KB, tile size, InReg,
WReg, and OutReg or IntReg respectively).
The same full grid of PE configurations used in CS1 is evaluated for
each register size, and the minimum-PE feasible mapping is recorded.
The goal is to establish which register types are binding for each
workload and to quantify the \emph{binding hierarchy} among the three
register types.

\paragraph{IntReg sweep (CS2).}
For the activation-dominant workloads, IntReg is non-binding.
FSRCNN yields the same minimum PE configuration ($128 \times 4$,
512~PEs) at all three tested sizes (200, 300, 400); energy scales
linearly with register size
($1.03 \times 10^{5}$ to $1.36 \times 10^{5}$\,$\mu$J) but no
additional PEs are needed, confirming that the weight register alone
determines feasibility.
MC-CNN saturates at IntReg\,$\ge$\,30: below this threshold
(IntReg\,=\,15) the footprint of 17~entries exceeds the capacity,
requiring 4\,096~PEs instead of the usual 1\,024.

For the weight-dominant workloads, IntReg is binding.
Taking ResNet18 as an example, the per-PE IntReg footprint is
209~entries at $\text{cols} = 16$.
At IntReg\,=\,50 the only feasible configurations have at least
128~PE columns, pushing the minimum array to 32\,768~PEs.
Doubling IntReg to 100 halves the required columns (64), and
doubling again to 200 halves them once more (32).
Saturation is reached at IntReg\,$\ge$\,200, where the IntReg
footprint fits within the register at the WReg-limited column count.
VGG16 behaves similarly, saturating at IntReg\,$\ge$\,230.

\paragraph{OutReg sweep (CS3).}
The output register is the weakest constraint in the binding
hierarchy.
For FSRCNN, energy scales linearly with OutReg size
($9.32 \times 10^{4}$ to $1.12 \times 10^{5}$\,$\mu$J), but the
minimum PE count is unchanged at 1\,024, confirming that OutReg is
non-binding.
MC-CNN is similarly unaffected: energy and latency remain constant
across all four tested sizes (8 to 64), and the EDP is flat at
$2.78 \times 10^{6}$\,J$\cdot$cc.

For the weight-dominant workloads, OutReg is weakly binding only at
very small sizes.
VGG16 requires 10\,752~PEs at OutReg\,=\,4 but saturates already at
OutReg\,$\ge$\,16 (4\,096~PEs).
ResNet18 follows the same pattern: OutReg\,=\,8 needs 16\,384~PEs, but
OutReg\,$\ge$\,16 saturates at 8\,192~PEs.
In both cases the saturation threshold is very low, because the
per-PE OutReg footprint is small (32~entries for VGG16, 8 for
ResNet18 at $\text{cols} = 16$) compared to the WReg footprint.

\paragraph{Binding hierarchy.}
Combining the results of CS1, CS2, and CS3, the register binding
hierarchy for the Eyeriss-Like architecture is:
%
\[
  \text{WReg} \;\gg\; \text{IntReg} \;>\; \text{OutReg}
\]
%
The weight register dominates because it stores the residual factors
of \emph{all} fused layers, whose cumulative size dwarfs the
intermediate and output footprints.
IntReg is a secondary constraint for weight-dominant workloads, where
large channel counts produce sizable intermediate activations.
OutReg is the weakest, reaching its saturation threshold at very
small register sizes.

\paragraph{Key takeaway.}
When sizing the per-PE registers for the Eyeriss-Like architecture
under full fusion, the weight register should be dimensioned first,
as it is the bottleneck for feasibility.
The intermediate register requires attention only for weight-dominant
workloads with deep fused groups, while the output register can be
set to a modest value (16--32 entries) without constraining the
minimum PE count.
