\subsection{Partial-Sized Architecture: Fusion Mode Comparison
  (Scenario~B)}
\label{sec:fusion-partial-sized}

\subsubsection{Motivation}

Scenario~A (Section~\ref{sec:fusion-full-sized}) sized the
architecture to support full fusion of the entire workload.
The resulting memories are large, particularly on DepFiN, where
VGG16 requires a 14.4\,MB weight memory and ResNet18 requires
10.7\,MB.
A natural follow-up question is: \emph{what happens if the
architecture is sized only for the largest pairwise (partial)
fusion, which needs substantially less on-chip storage?}

Scenario~B answers this by building a smaller architecture for
each workload, just large enough to fuse any two consecutive
layers, and then comparing partial-fusion execution against
single-layer execution on that same hardware.
Full fusion is not attempted, because the reduced memories
cannot accommodate it.
Table~\ref{tab:partial-sized-configs} lists the resulting
configurations.

The memory reductions are striking for the weight-dominant
workloads on DepFiN.
VGG16's weight memory drops from 14\,366\,KB (Scenario~A) to
4\,610\,KB, a $3.1\times$ reduction; ResNet18 drops from
10\,738\,KB to 4\,608\,KB ($2.3\times$).
On Eyeriss, VGG16's WReg falls from 1\,200 to 576 words per PE,
and ResNet18's WReg from 902 to 384 words.
The activation-dominant workloads see smaller but still
meaningful reductions on DepFiN (FSRCNN fmem from 576\,KB to
248\,KB, wmem from 19\,KB to 9\,KB), while on Eyeriss their
configuration is unchanged because the GlobalBuffer and WReg
were already set at the pairwise minimum.

\subsubsection{Results}

Figure~\ref{fig:partial-sized-norm} presents the normalised
comparison (partial fusion\,=\,1.0) for energy, latency, and EDP.

\paragraph{Energy.}
As in Scenario~A, partial fusion is always the more
energy-expensive mode.
On DepFiN, the partial-to-single energy ratio ranges from
$1.33\times$ (FSRCNN) to $15.1\times$ (VGG16).
On Eyeriss the ratios are larger, from $6.5\times$ (FSRCNN) to
$16.8\times$ (ResNet18), reflecting the per-PE register
replication cost discussed in Section~\ref{sec:fusion-full-sized}.

Compared with Scenario~A, the energy penalty of fusion is
\emph{reduced} because the smaller memories lower the per-access
cost.
For instance, DepFiN VGG16's fusion-to-single energy ratio drops
from $22.3\times$ (full fusion, Scenario~A) to $15.1\times$
(partial fusion, Scenario~B), and ResNet18 drops from
$11.4\times$ to $10.1\times$.
The improvement is less dramatic on Eyeriss because the
GlobalBuffer size is unchanged and the WReg reduction is moderate.

\paragraph{Latency.}
The latency pattern mirrors Scenario~A closely.
Partial fusion saves $45.9\%$ on FSRCNN and $41.5\%$ on MC-CNN
for DepFiN, comparable to the $47.6\%$ and $42.3\%$ savings of
full fusion in Scenario~A.
On Eyeriss the activation-dominant savings are identical to
Scenario~A ($45.6\%$ for FSRCNN, $15.7\%$ for MC-CNN), because
the architecture configuration has not changed for these
workloads.

For the weight-dominant workloads the latency picture changes
only at the margins.
On DepFiN, VGG16 is now essentially latency-neutral ($0.8\%$
saving), a marked improvement over the $-21.1\%$ penalty of
full fusion in Scenario~A, because the smaller weight memory
reduces the scheduling overhead.
ResNet18 remains slower under fusion ($-13.1\%$), though the
penalty is less severe than the $-30.3\%$ in Scenario~A.
On Eyeriss, VGG16 and ResNet18 retain the same modest savings
($15.5\%$ and $4.9\%$) observed in Scenario~A.

\paragraph{EDP.}
The EDP verdict is unambiguous.
On DepFiN, partial fusion wins for FSRCNN only: its EDP
($5.41 \times 10^{4}$) is $28\%$ lower than the single-layer sum
($7.51 \times 10^{4}$).
For all other DepFiN workloads, single-layer execution dominates,
with MC-CNN single at $0.569\times$ partial, VGG16 at
$0.067\times$, and ResNet18 at $0.088\times$.

On Eyeriss, single-layer execution wins for every workload
without exception.
The normalised single-layer EDP is $0.286$ for FSRCNN, $0.112$
for MC-CNN, $0.071$ for VGG16, and $0.063$ for ResNet18.
Even the FSRCNN case, which benefits from a large latency saving,
cannot overcome the $6.5\times$ energy penalty.

\paragraph{Comparison with Scenario~A.}
Scenario~B demonstrates that sizing the architecture for pairwise
fusion rather than full fusion brings two advantages and one
unchanged limitation.

The first advantage is the substantial memory reduction
(Table~\ref{tab:partial-sized-configs}), which lowers chip area
and fabrication cost.
The second is that the latency benefit of fusion is almost fully
preserved: partial fusion captures $92$--$98\%$ of the
full-fusion latency saving for the activation-dominant workloads,
and it eliminates the worst-case latency \emph{penalty} that full
fusion imposed on DepFiN VGG16 ($-21\%{\to}+0.8\%$).

The unchanged limitation is the energy overhead.
Although the smaller memories reduce the absolute energy, partial
fusion in Scenario~B is still $1.3$--$16.8\times$ more expensive
than single-layer execution, so the EDP winner remains
single-layer in seven of the eight architecture--workload
combinations (the exception is FSRCNN on DepFiN).

\paragraph{Key takeaway.}
A partial-fusion-sized architecture delivers nearly the same
latency benefit as a full-fusion architecture, at a fraction of
the memory cost.
The energy--EDP trade-off, however, is unchanged: the on-chip
memory overhead of fusing even two layers still dominates the EDP
comparison, making single-layer execution the more efficient
choice in all but the most activation-heavy workload on DepFiN.
