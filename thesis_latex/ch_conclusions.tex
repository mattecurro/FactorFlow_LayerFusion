\section{Summary}
\label{sec:concl-summary}

This thesis extended the FactorFlow analytical mapping framework with
support for fused multi-layer DNN execution on spatial architectures.
The work encompassed modelling, implementation, validation, and an
extensive experimental evaluation.
This section recapitulates the key findings along two axes: the
impact of layer fusion as an execution strategy, and the role that
FactorFlow plays in enabling its analysis.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Impact of Layer Fusion}
\label{sec:concl-fusion-impact}

The central premise of layer fusion is that eliminating the
intermediate-activation round-trips through external memory can
reduce both energy and latency.
The experiments conducted on two spatial architectures (DepFiN-like
and Eyeriss-v1-like) and four CNN workloads (FSRCNN, MC-CNN, VGG16,
ResNet18) confirm part of this premise and, equally importantly,
reveal its boundaries.

\paragraph{DRAM traffic reduction.}
Full fusion achieves its primary objective: DRAM traffic is reduced
by 25--95\% depending on the workload, with activation-dominant
workloads (FSRCNN, MC-CNN) benefiting the most because their
intermediate activations constitute the overwhelming majority of
off-chip traffic in single-layer mode.

\paragraph{Latency benefit.}
The DRAM-traffic reduction translates into substantial latency
improvements for activation-dominant workloads: 42--48\% on DepFiN
and 16--46\% on Eyeriss.
For weight-dominant workloads the results are more nuanced.
On Eyeriss, full fusion still reduces latency (7--21\%), but on
DepFiN it can be \emph{slower} than single-layer execution (up to
30\% latency increase) because the very large fused weight memory
requires additional temporal scheduling passes that outweigh the
DRAM savings.
Partial (pairwise) fusion captures 87--103\% of the full-fusion
latency benefit, indicating that nearly all the inter-layer traffic
saving is realised by fusing just two consecutive layers.

\paragraph{Energy overhead.}
Full fusion is the most energy-expensive mode in every
architecture--workload combination tested.
The energy penalty ranges from a modest $1.3\times$ (FSRCNN on
DepFiN) to a severe $36.9\times$ (VGG16 on Eyeriss).
The cause is the enlarged on-chip memory: fusing all layers demands
substantially more local storage, and every on-chip data access
passes through these bigger memories, whose per-access energy
scales with capacity.
The Eyeriss architecture suffers the most because its per-PE
register is replicated across all processing elements, amplifying
the aggregate dynamic energy.

\paragraph{Energy--Delay Product.}
When energy and latency are combined into the EDP metric, full
fusion wins in only one of the eight architecture--workload
combinations (FSRCNN on DepFiN, where the $47.6\%$ latency saving
outweighs the modest $1.3\times$ energy penalty).
In all other cases, single-layer execution is the more efficient
choice.
This result clarifies the role of layer fusion:
\emph{fusion is primarily a latency optimisation, not an energy
optimisation}.
Its benefit grows with the activation-to-weight traffic ratio and
shrinks as the on-chip memory overhead increases.
Whether the trade-off is acceptable depends on the design priority:
throughput-critical deployments may prefer the latency reduction,
while energy-constrained systems should favour single-layer
execution.

\paragraph{Partial fusion as a practical compromise.}
Scenario~B showed that an architecture sized for pairwise fusion
requires substantially less on-chip memory than a full-fusion
design, yet captures nearly the same latency benefit.
The energy--EDP trade-off remains unfavourable in most cases, but
the reduced chip area and fabrication cost make partial fusion the
more realistic deployment option for current accelerator designs.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Impact of Using FactorFlow}
\label{sec:concl-factorflow-impact}

The choice to extend FactorFlow, rather than building a new tool or
adapting Timeloop, proved instrumental in several respects.

\paragraph{Generality of the factor-based abstraction.}
FactorFlow's representation of the map space as a set of factors
distributed across hierarchy levels and dimensions generalised
naturally to the fused-layer setting.
The aliasing mechanism (shared activation dimensions) and the
decoupling mechanism (per-layer weight dimensions) were expressed
entirely within the existing factor vocabulary, without requiring
changes to the core data structures.

\paragraph{Extended Einsum notation.}
The use of the extended Einsum notation~\cite{een} as the workload
representation allowed the framework to capture fused convolutions,
including affine dimension coupling and stride-based cascading tile
dependencies, without workload-specific code paths.
This makes the tool applicable to any CONV-like layer topology
expressible in Einsum form.

\paragraph{Cost-model extensibility.}
The single-layer cost model (MOPs, energy via Accelergy, latency
with bandwidth-limited stall detection) was generalised to a
per-layer decomposition with modest code effort.
The resulting model correctly reproduces the DepFiN accelerator's
behaviour: ideal latency of 37.94\,M cycles matches the
compute-bound reference exactly, DRAM bypass of intermediate
operands is enforced with zero intermediate DRAM traffic, and
on-chip memory stalls are negligible (24~cycles out of 37.94\,M,
arising only from the boundary layers of the fusion pipeline).

\paragraph{Map-space pruning.}
The depth-first scheduling heuristic, enforced at every hierarchy
level through the cascading tile-size inequality, prunes infeasible
mappings before cost evaluation.
Without such pruning, the fused-layer map space---exceeding
$10^{463}$ for an 8-layer FSRCNN fusion---would be entirely
intractable, even for fast analytical models.
The heuristic reduces the effective search space to a manageable
subspace of depth-first-compatible schedules, enabling the mapper
to find valid configurations within seconds.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:concl-future}

While the framework developed in this thesis demonstrates the
feasibility of analytical fused-layer mapping exploration, several
limitations remain that define clear directions for future research.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Automated Mapper for Fused Workloads}
\label{sec:fw-mapper}

The most impactful limitation is the absence of an automated mapper
for multi-layer fused workloads
(Section~\ref{sec:ps-limitations}).
Currently, fused mappings must be specified manually through the
architecture definition file, which limits the exploration to
expert-guided configurations.
Extending the existing mapper infrastructure---linear, quadratic,
and hybrid search---to automatically explore the fused map space,
while respecting the depth-first heuristic constraints at every
move, would transform the tool from an evaluator of prescribed
mappings into a true design-space exploration engine.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Inter-Layer Parallelism}
\label{sec:fw-inter-layer}

The current model enforces sequential layer execution: all layers
share the same PE array and execute one at a time.
Architectures that exploit \emph{pipeline parallelism}, where
different layers compute concurrently on disjoint subsets of the
PE array, are not modelled.
Supporting inter-layer parallelism would require extending the
latency model to capture overlapping execution intervals and the
associated buffer-management complexity, but would enable the
framework to evaluate a broader class of fused-layer schedules.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Formal Verification of the Heuristic}
\label{sec:fw-heuristic}

The depth-first scheduling heuristic is a necessary but not
sufficient condition for mapping validity: it rules out many
infeasible mappings but does not guarantee that every accepted
mapping is fully correct.
Formal verification---proving that all mappings accepted by the
heuristic produce valid schedules for any architecture and workload
configuration---would strengthen the theoretical foundation of
the approach and eliminate the need for post-hoc validation of
individual mappings.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Innermost Dimension-Sum Reuse}
\label{sec:fw-dimsum}

When two dimensions contribute to the same operand index through
an affine sum (e.g.\ $y + r$ in a convolution), the current model
does not exploit the overlap between consecutive iterations of the
sliding window within a single tile.
This leads to conservative (over-estimated) MOPs counts at the
innermost memory level.
Implementing fine-grained footprint analysis that accounts for
the sliding-window overlap would improve the accuracy of the
energy estimates, particularly for workloads with large filter
sizes.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Generalised Memory-Port Model}
\label{sec:fw-memory-port}

The special-case treatment of the DepFiN Feature Memory's latency
(summing read and write latencies rather than taking the maximum)
is currently hardcoded in the cost model.
A more general parameterisation of memory-port behaviour---covering
single-port, dual-port, and banked configurations---would improve
the portability of the framework to architectures beyond the
DepFiN template.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Support for Non-Sequential Layer Topologies}
\label{sec:fw-residual}

The current framework assumes a strictly sequential layer chain,
where each layer consumes the output of its immediate predecessor.
Modern DNN architectures frequently employ residual (skip)
connections, concatenation operators, and multi-branch topologies.
Extending the aliasing mechanism to model fan-in and fan-out
connections would enable the framework to handle architectures
such as ResNet (skip connections) and Inception (parallel branches)
in their native form, rather than requiring the user to decompose
them into sequential segments.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Porting to a Compiled Language}
\label{sec:fw-compiled}

Porting FactorFlow to a compiled systems programming language,
such as C++ or Rust, represents a highly promising avenue for
future work.
The current Python implementation, while effective for prototyping
and validation, incurs significant interpreter overhead and is
constrained by the Global Interpreter Lock (GIL), which prevents
true shared-memory multithreading.
Transitioning to a compiled environment would eliminate interpreter
overhead and enable the adoption of lightweight, shared-memory
multithreading over process-based concurrency, significantly
reducing execution time and system overhead.
This improvement is particularly relevant for the automated mapper:
a compiled back-end could evaluate millions of candidate mappings
per second, making exhaustive or near-exhaustive search of the
pruned fused-layer map space practical for moderately sized
workloads.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Integration with Graph-Level Scheduling}
\label{sec:fw-graph-level}

The framework currently operates at the \emph{dataflow level},
optimising the mapping of a fixed set of fused layers.
A complementary direction is \emph{graph-level} scheduling, which
decides \emph{which} layers to fuse and how to partition the
network graph into fusion segments.
Tools like Optimus~\cite{Optimus} address this partitioning
problem but rely on coarse cost estimates.
Coupling a graph-level scheduler with the fine-grained analytical
model developed in this thesis would enable joint optimisation of
the fusion topology and the dataflow mapping, potentially
unlocking configurations that neither level can discover in
isolation.
